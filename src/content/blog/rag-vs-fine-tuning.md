When firms want AI that actually knows *their* business, two approaches come up: retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and fine-tuning. They solve overlapping problems in very different ways.

## What RAG does

RAG keeps your knowledge in a searchable store and feeds the most relevant pieces to the model at question time. The model stays general; your data stays separate and easy to update.

**Best when:**

- Your information changes often
- You need answers grounded in specific, citable sources
- You want to add or remove knowledge without retraining

## What fine-tuning does

Fine-tuning bakes patterns directly into the model's weights. It's powerful for teaching a consistent style, format, or specialized task, but it's heavier to maintain.

**Best when:**

- You need a very specific tone or output structure
- The underlying knowledge rarely changes
- You have clean, labeled training examples

## A simple rule of thumb

| Need | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Up-to-date, citable answers | RAG |
| Consistent style or format | Fine-tuning |
| Both | RAG first, fine-tune later |

For most professional services firms, **RAG is the right first move**: lower cost, faster to ship, and far easier to keep current.
